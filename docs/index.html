How "Add to Zotero" works

The button copies the article's DOI. Then, in Zotero:

  • Desktop app: click the magic-wand button (Add Item by Identifier), paste, and press Enter. Zotero fetches the full record.
  • Web library, including on your phone: open zotero.org/mylibrary, choose "Add by identifier" and paste.
  • Several at once: tick the boxes on the cards, then "Copy DOIs for Zotero". Paste them all into the same box; Zotero accepts one per line.
  • Zotero Connector: open the DOI link in your browser and click the Zotero button in the toolbar.

What is collected. Three topics, searched separately: brain stimulation (TMS, TMS-EEG, TUS, DBS, tDCS, tACS), executive functions (including cognitive control, cognitive flexibility, set shifting, task switching, inhibitory control, response inhibition and working memory) and the basal ganglia (including striatum, caudate, putamen, globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra and nucleus accumbens). Papers that combine stimulation with executive functions or the basal ganglia are "core matches" and are all included. For each topic on its own, the six most relevant papers of the week are summarised; the box above links to the full weekly lists on PubMed.

Sources. PubMed, bioRxiv (neuroscience) and medRxiv (neurology, psychiatry and rehabilitation). Preprints are not peer reviewed. Google Scholar is not included.

Updates. A scheduled task refreshes the page every Monday morning (IST). Opening the page shows the last update; it does not search when opened. Articles stay for 12 weeks.

Attribution. Article details come from PubMed (NCBI), bioRxiv and medRxiv records. Each entry links to its DOI.

Summaries. Written by Claude from each abstract, in its own words. Tags are assigned by Claude. Check the paper before citing.

Read and Zotero marks. On claude.ai they are kept in your private space on this page, so they follow you across devices. On the public copy (rajatjoshi15.github.io) they are saved in this browser only. Nobody else can see them either way.
